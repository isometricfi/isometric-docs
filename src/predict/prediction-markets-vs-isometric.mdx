# Prediction markets versus Isometric

A listed prediction market, such as Polymarket, offers questions someone else wrote: will Bitcoin be above $105,000 on Friday? You pick from the prices and dates on the list. On Isometric you write the question yourself.

## Side by side

|                       | Listed prediction market                 | Isometric                                     |
| --------------------- | ---------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------- |
| **The price**         | A few preset levels                      | Any price writers quote                       |
| **The end time**      | Preset dates                             | Whole-hour terms from 12 hours to 7 days      |
| **Fills**             | As deep as the order book                | Writers quote your whole amount at once       |
| **Payout**            | Fixed per share                          | Fixed when you place it                       |
| **Most you can lose** | What you paid                            | What you paid                                 |
| **Exit**              | Usually any time, by selling your shares | Held to the end time                          |
| **What decides it**   | The market's own resolution rules        | One Chainlink price at the end time, on chain |

## Where Isometric fits better

- **You have a view on your own line.** BTC above $103,500 by Tuesday at 18:00 is a prediction you can place, even if nobody listed it.
- **You want one price for the whole amount.** There is no book to walk through.
- **You want a clear result.** One onchain price settles it, with no dispute or vote.

## Where a listed market fits better

- **You want to exit early.** You can usually sell your shares before the end. An Isometric prediction runs to its end time.
- **You want to bet on something other than a price.** Isometric only predicts market prices.
