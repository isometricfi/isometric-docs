# What leverage means here

On a perp, leverage is how much you borrow, and it comes with a price at which you get wiped out. On Isometric there is no borrowing and no liquidation. Leverage does two things: it sets how big your position is, and where your target sits.

## Leverage sets position size

Your position size is your amount times the leverage. It is currently capped at **5,000 USDG** per trade, so higher leverage lowers the most you can enter.

| Largest amount | Leverage | Position size |
| -------------- | -------- | ------------- |
| $1,000         | 5x       | $5,000        |
| $500           | 10x      | $5,000        |
| $200           | 25x      | $5,000        |
| $100           | 50x      | $5,000        |
| $50            | 100x     | $5,000        |
| $5             | 1000x    | $5,000        |

Leverage comes in eleven tiers: 5x, 10x, 15x, 20x, 25x, 30x, 40x, 50x, 75x, 100x, and 1000x.

## Leverage moves your target

Paying less for the same position means more risk. Higher leverage moves your **Worthless at or below** price closer to today's price, and from 50x past it, so less has to go wrong before the position is worth nothing.

Six three-day Bitcoin longs with Bitcoin at $100,000, each with a $5,000 position size:

| Leverage | Amount | Worthless at or below | Profit above | Max payout |
| -------- | ------ | --------------------- | ------------ | ---------- |
| 5x       | $1,000 | $83,738               | $100,486     | $1,568     |
| 10x      | $500   | $91,402               | $100,542     | $1,017     |
| 25x      | $200   | $97,388               | $101,283     | $648       |
| 50x      | $100   | $100,466              | $102,476     | $474       |
| 100x     | $50    | $102,936              | $103,965     | $343       |
| 1000x    | $5     | $108,283              | $108,391     | $79        |

These are example figures. The exact prices move with the market and the ticket shows yours.

Read across a row and the trade at each tier is clear:

| Leverage | Where the worthless price sits | What a small move against you does | Payout per 1% past target |
| -------- | ------------------------------ | ---------------------------------- | ------------------------- |
| 5x       | Far on the safe side of today  | You get most of your money back    | 5% of your amount         |
| 10x      | Closer                         | You get some of it back            | 10%                       |
| 25x      | Close to today's price         | You can lose all of it             | 25%                       |
| 50x      | Past today's price             | It needs a move your way to pay    | 50%                       |
| 100x     | Further past today's price     | It needs a bigger move your way    | 100%                      |
| 1000x    | Near the Max payout price      | It needs a large move your way     | 1,000%                    |

At low leverage the position behaves like a small, safe stake in the price: it is rarely worthless and rarely pays many times over. At high leverage a small move in your favour pays a lot and a small move against you costs the whole premium.

The exact distances move with the market. When the market is calm the targets sit closer. When it is volatile they sit further away, because a big move is more likely and writers price it in.

## Leverage does not create a liquidation price

Nothing happens to your position while it is open. If a 25x long goes 10% against you on day one and recovers on day two, it pays in full on day three. A perp at the same leverage would have been closed out on day one.

The cost of that protection is already inside what you paid. There is no margin to top up and no funding to pay.

## Breakeven and the cap

- **Breakeven usually sits a little past today's price.** Each 1% past your worthless price pays back leverage percent of your amount, so breakeven is one leverage-share past the worthless price.
- **The payout is capped.** The ticket shows the **Max payout** in dollars. Longer terms have a larger one.

## Choosing a leverage

Ask where you think the price will be at settlement, not how much you want to make.

- If you think it will drift your way or hold, pick low leverage. You keep most of your money across a wide range of outcomes.
- If you think it will move decisively, pick high leverage. Each 1% of the move pays more.
- If you are not sure, the middle tiers split the difference.

The ticket shows exactly where each tier puts the target. Move the slider and watch the worthless price, the profit price, and the max payout shift before you decide.
