# Reading the quote

Every quote shows the same six numbers. They do not change meaning between markets, terms, or leverage. Once you can read them, you can read any Isometric trade.

## The six numbers

| Number                    | What it tells you                                                                                                               |
| ------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Settles**               | The date and hour the position ends. One price at that hour decides the result. It cannot be closed before then.                |
| **Position size**         | Your amount times leverage, or a little more. The exposure the position gives you.                                              |
| **Worthless at or below** | For a long: settle at or below this price and you receive nothing. A short shows **Worthless at or above**.                     |
| **Profit above**          | For a long: settle above this price and you get back more than you paid. A short shows **Profit below**.                        |
| **Max payout**            | The most the position can pay in USDG. It is fixed when the trade opens.                                                        |
| **Max loss**              | Your amount, paid now including the trading fee. If the position expires worthless you lose it. No margin call, no liquidation. |

Between the worthless price and the profit price the position pays something, but less than it cost. That band is where you get part of your money back.

## Max payout

Every position has a maximum payout, fixed when you open it. A long earns on the way up from its worthless price to its cap price and nothing beyond. A short earns on the way down from its worthless price to its floor price and nothing beyond.

The cap sits further from today's price on a longer term and in a more volatile market, so a 7-day position has more room to run than a 1-day one. The ticket shows the dollar amount before you confirm. Once the position is open, its payoff chart in Portfolio goes flat at the price where the payout stops, and you can drag across the chart to read the payout at any settlement price. The cap never changes after the trade opens.

The cap is what lets the pool set aside the whole **Max payout** for you on day one instead of an open-ended amount.

## A worked example

Bitcoin is at **$100,000**. You go **Long**, enter **$500**, choose **7 days** and **25x**. The ticket might show:

| Number                | Value                        |
| --------------------- | ---------------------------- |
| Settles               | 7 days from now, on the hour |
| Position size         | $12,686                      |
| Worthless at or below | $97,680                      |
| Profit above          | $101,529                     |
| Max payout            | $3,315.97                    |
| Max loss              | $500.00                      |

The exact figures depend on the live price, how volatile the market has been, and how busy the pool is, so yours will differ. This position's payout stops growing at **$123,211**.

Settlement at different prices pays:

| Bitcoin at settlement | You receive | Result  |
| --------------------- | ----------- | ------- |
| $97,680 or below      | $0          | −$500   |
| $101,000              | $431        | −$69    |
| $101,529              | $500        | $0      |
| $105,000              | $951        | +$451   |
| $110,000              | $1,600      | +$1,100 |
| $120,000              | $2,899      | +$2,399 |
| $123,211 and above    | $3,316      | +$2,816 |

Two things to notice. Between $97,680 and $101,529 you get some money back but not all of it: with Bitcoin unchanged at $100,000 you receive $301. And the payout stops growing at $123,211: Bitcoin at $150,000 pays the same $3,316.

## The same idea as a short

Bitcoin is at $100,000. You go **Short**, enter **$400**, choose **1 day** and **25x**. The ticket might show:

| Number                | Value     |
| --------------------- | --------- |
| Position size         | $10,436   |
| Worthless at or above | $103,712  |
| Profit below          | $99,737   |
| Max payout            | $1,270.72 |
| Max loss              | $400.00   |

This position's payout stops growing at **$91,084**.

| Bitcoin at settlement | You receive | Result |
| --------------------- | ----------- | ------ |
| $103,712 or above     | $0          | −$400  |
| $101,000              | $273        | −$127  |
| $99,737               | $400        | $0     |
| $98,000               | $575        | +$175  |
| $95,000               | $877        | +$477  |
| $91,084 and below     | $1,271      | +$871  |

In both examples the worthless price sits on the safe side of today's price and the profit price sits just past it. That is typical: a position starts with some value, and you come out ahead once the price moves a little further your way.

## Two shortcuts

**Each 1% past the worthless price is worth at least leverage percent of your amount.** In the long example, every $1,000 above $97,680 adds about $130 to the payout, which is about 26% of the $500 you entered.

**Breakeven is the Profit above price**, or **Profit below** for a short. It is never more than one leverage-share past the worthless price: 4% at 25x, 10% at 10x, 20% at 5x.

## The quote follows the market

The quote updates as the price moves. If the price moves before your trade lands, the trade either opens close to the quote you saw or fails. A failed trade costs only the network fee, and an open one costs exactly the amount you entered.

The **Max payout** and the price it stops at are set from the live price when the trade lands, so they can differ slightly from the quote if the price ticks in between.
