# Direction, amount, term, and leverage

The ticket has four controls. Together they describe a position. Everything else on the ticket is calculated from them.

## Direction

**Long** pays if the settlement price is above your target. **Short** pays if it is below.

You pay for the right to be paid if the price finishes on your side of a line, with no borrowing and no margin. Both directions are paid for in USDG and pay out in USDG.

## Amount

The USDG you are willing to spend on the position:

- It sets your **position size** together with leverage.
- It is the **most you can lose**. Nothing is ever taken from you after this.
- It is paid **once**, at open. There is no funding while you hold, and no fee when the position settles.

At launch the amount is between **$10 and $10,000** per position. A single position also cannot be more than a small share of the pool, and the ticket tells you if you are over it.

You are charged exactly the amount you enter, fee included. The ticket shows it as **Max loss**.

## Term

How long until the position settles: **1, 3, 7, or 30 days**. The ticket shows the exact date and hour, because settlement lands on a fixed schedule rather than exactly one day from the moment you click.

Term changes the trade in three ways:

- **More time costs more.** For the same amount and leverage, a longer term pushes your **Profit above** price further from today's price, because each unit of exposure is more expensive when it has longer to work.
- **More time to be right.** A 7-day position survives two days of going the wrong way. A 1-day position does not get that chance.
- **More room to run.** The price at which the payout stops growing sits further away on a longer term, so the **Max payout** is larger.

There is no way to close before the term ends. Pick a term you are happy to wait out.

## Leverage

**5x, 10x, 25x, 50x, or 100x.** Leverage sets how much a move pays. Enter $500 at 25x and you hold a position of at least $12,500, so every 1% past your target is worth at least $125. Lower the leverage and each 1% is worth less.

Higher leverage also means higher risk. Your **Worthless at or below** price moves closer to today's price, so a smaller move against you leaves the position with nothing, and your **Profit above** price usually sits a little further away. [What leverage means here](./leverage.mdx) shows every tier side by side.

Leverage does **not** create a liquidation price. A 25x position that goes the wrong way for six days and comes back on the seventh pays out in full.

## How the four fit together

| You change      | Position size | Worthless price             | Profit price                  | Payout per 1% move past target | Max payout |
| --------------- | ------------- | --------------------------- | ----------------------------- | ------------------------------ | ---------- |
| Larger amount   | Bigger        | Same                        | Same                          | Bigger                         | Bigger     |
| Longer term     | Same          | Moves against you, or stays | Further away                  | Same                           | Bigger     |
| Higher leverage | Bigger        | Moves against you           | Usually a little further away | Bigger                         | Bigger     |

**Every 1% the price settles past your worthless price is worth leverage percent of your amount**, until the payout reaches its maximum. At 25x, each 1% is 25% of your amount. At 5x, each 1% is 5%.
